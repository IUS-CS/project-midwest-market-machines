# User Scenarios

## James’s Scenarios

### Scenario 1: James’ Day Off

James starts his day at 6:00 with some bacon and eggs, followed by a quick surf session and gets ready for a consulting session with an automotive robotics firm in Germany. After his technical work is done and their questions are sorted, James doesn’t want to stare at candles or ticker tapes; he wants to be present with his family.

Wasting no time, James logs into the Midwest Marketing Machine web app and makes a deposit from a recent check available to the pool. Seeing a connection with his current work, he directs the AI to watch assets related to Substack articles he has been following.

He identifies three emerging stocks in the automation space and adds them to his AI watchlist. Rather than manually setting buy and sell orders, he sets risk parameters that instruct the AI to watch market fluctuations and buy at thresholds that correlate with news on his Substack account, connecting his technical knowledge of the industry’s progress to the AI’s automated watch, purchase, sell, and record lifecycle.

Once these instructions are set, James closes his laptop. For the next six hours, he is entirely offline, with his best-informed investment effort and an automated execution of it. While he is going about his business, the Midwest Marketing Machine is actively monitoring the market.

When James checks the app briefly before dinner, he sees his available cash, stocks, and their value in the portfolio breakdown screen. He scrolls to a stack of actions that tells him exactly what the AI executed in his absence. He notes that the machine bought a dip in a robotics firm while he was out. Satisfied that his capital is working as hard as he used to, James logs off to spend the evening with his kids.

## Rick’s Scenarios

### Scenario 2: Rick’s Olympic Gains

Rick is a physical therapist assistant in Elon, North Carolina, who balances a busy 9-to-5 job with nightly studying to become a Doctor of Physical Therapy. Rick wants to grow his savings, but has heard of weakening confidence in the U.S. dollar along with AI bubble fears, and wants to be able to use his money regularly instead of long-term index funds.

After seeing an advertisement for the Midwest Marketing Machine web app, Rick goes on his work computer and decides to give it a try. Since he has to manage his tuition finances, he makes a small investment of $500 into his wallet.

After browsing the featured page and seeing mostly AI stocks, Rick uses the search bar to search categories he thinks might be related to the Olympics. He searches for companies behind the streaming services he’s been watching and learns that Comcast owns NBC and Peacock, and favorites the stock for later.

Rather than guessing when to buy, Rick toggles the AI watchlist feature for the Comcast stock and confirms a purchase of five shares. The system primes to buy shares and waits for a downturn.

A week later, the Olympics end and the closing ceremony reminds him of his position. Rick checks the performance and sees that his $500 has grown into $521, generating a modest 4% return in a week while he focused on his patients and studies.
